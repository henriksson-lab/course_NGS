# Figure audit: the rules

Every figure in this course is generated by a `make-figs.py` from primitives in
`tools/figlib.py`. They have been checked for slide height, for missing files and for text
leaving the canvas — but until now nobody had looked at them all at full size. A reader found
overlapping labels in a published figure, and a sweep then found fourteen more faults of the
same kind, one of which was a label painted over completely by an opaque box.

This audit looks at every figure in your units, at full size, against the rules below.

## How to work

Render each figure and **look at it**. `cairosvg` is installed:

```
python3 -c "import cairosvg; cairosvg.svg2png(url='unit/fig01-x.svg', write_to='/tmp/f.png', scale=1.6)"
```

`python3 tools/check_figures.py <unit>` gives you a geometry triage list. Treat it as a prior,
not a verdict: it over-reports (a label inside a box is fine) and it is blind to everything in
rules 3 to 7. A figure it does not mention can still be wrong.

Fix faults by editing `make-figs.py`. **Never edit an SVG by hand** — it is generated and your
edit will be overwritten. Never run git.

## The rules

**1. Legibility.** No text printed over other text. No text printed over a filled shape it is
not a label for — a label centred in its own box is correct, a label hanging across the edge
of someone else's box is not. Nothing may leave the canvas. Nothing below about 9 px.

**2. Attachment.** Every label must be unambiguously attached to the thing it labels. If the
nearest mark to a label is not its subject, the reader will attach it wrongly. Where a label
cannot sit next to its subject, use a leader line — `long-reads/fig21-platform-compare.svg` is
the worked example of that fix.

**3. Correctness.** This matters more than tidiness.
- Sequences must base-pair. `duplex()` asserts this, but strings written by hand into `seq()`
  or `t()` do not get checked.
- 5′ and 3′ must be the right way round, and an arrow showing synthesis must point 5′→3′.
- Numbers in the figure must agree with the numbers on the slide that shows it, and with the
  rest of the course.
- Colour carries meaning here: `TOP`/`BOT` for the two strands, `HOT` for the thing being
  pointed at, `OK` for what worked, `BAD` for the failure, `MUTE` for secondary detail. A
  figure that uses `BAD` for something that did not fail is lying quietly.

**4. It must teach.** A figure should show a mechanism — the molecule, the enzyme's action,
the failure mode — and should be readable without the slide's bullets. Decoration is a fault.

**5. House style.** Caption line at the top, note line at the bottom, figlib's font sizes,
roughly two to four times wider than tall, under about 300 px tall.

**6. Honesty.** Illustrative numbers must say they are illustrative. Several figures already
do this well ("the curves are illustrative; calibrate", "simulated, not a vendor figure").

**7. No recommendations.** The course states what a chemistry does, costs and fails, and
leaves the choice to the reader. A figure that tells the reader what to prefer breaks that.

## What to change, and what to report

Fix what is clearly wrong. Prefer moving a label, then shortening it, then growing the canvas;
dropping information is a last resort and must be reported.

Report rather than fix when the problem is substantive — a figure that is wrong about the
biology, or that duplicates another unit's figure, or that you think should not exist. A
needless change to a correct figure is worse than leaving a tool's false positive alone.

If you remove a figure from a build list every later figure is renumbered and every reference
after it breaks, so do not: appending is safe, removing is not.

## Before you finish

```
python3 <unit>/make-figs.py     # exits 0, for each unit you touched
python3 tools/check_course.py   # prints OK
```

Re-render and look at every figure you changed.

Report: how many figures you reviewed, each fault found with what you did about it, each
geometry hit you judged a false positive, and anything substantive you are flagging rather
than fixing.
